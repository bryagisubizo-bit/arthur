# Endangered-Language Expression Verification Sources

Arthur does not infer that an expression is correct from a language name, a broad geographic label, a translation model, or a social-media post. An expression can receive the in-app **Source-confirmed** state only when its record identifies a recognised source, a language or dialect, a regional/community context, an intended use context, and a sensitivity note. This state means that Arthur has retained the supplied source evidence; it does **not** represent community endorsement, fluent-speaker review, ownership, publication permission, or automatic permission to use the expression in conversation.

| Source profile | Evidence available for a source-confirmed record | Regional and contextual boundary | Arthur use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cherokee Nation Language Department | Official Cherokee Nation Language Department description and maintained Cherokee word list. | The entry must identify the relevant Cherokee language/community or dialect context instead of treating a spelling as universal. | The app may prepare a local evidence record, but keeps the expression unavailable to automatic speech, translation, search, or reply generation. |
| Learn Manx / Culture Vannin | The `1000 Words` learner resource identifies Manx material associated with the Isle of Man and gives `Aigh vie` as “Good luck.” | This is a beginner-learning source and a specific Island of Man cultural/learning context; it is not a general-purpose colloquial authority. | The bundled illustration may be presented only as a source-confirmed learning expression with its source and limitation visible. |
| Government of Canada, Canadian Heritage | The `How to say “hello” in Indigenous languages` page provides language and dialect-labelled examples for “Hello! How are you?” | The exact listed language and dialect label must remain attached. A national government page is not a substitute for a particular community’s ongoing approval. | Eligible for a source-confirmed record only; the app cannot call it community-reviewed or enable automatic use. |

## Verification rule

> **Source-confirmed** means the evidence fields have been checked against a permitted source profile. It is deliberately lower than **community-reviewed** and cannot be used by Arthur to speak, translate, search, publish, train on, or respond with the expression.

Permitted sources are a relevant language community or nation, a government or cultural institution, an academic/educational resource with clear authorship, or a recognised archival/lexical resource. User-entered links are never treated as verified automatically. They require a human confirmation step and may be rejected when the source does not establish the relevant language, region/dialect, or use context.
