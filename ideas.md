# Arthur Live Preview — Design Exploration

## Three Directions

### 1. Orbital Command Atelier
**Very Brief Intro:** A midnight command desk with cobalt instrumentation, measured glass surfaces, and an orbit-like spatial system. It should feel composed, discreet, and technically assured rather than theatrical.

**Probability:** 0.07

### 2. Warm Study Intelligence
**Very Brief Intro:** A quiet editorial workspace with parchment tones, brass accents, and library-card organization. It frames Arthur as an intimate, thoughtful companion rather than a computer console.

**Probability:** 0.04

### 3. Kinetic Signal Field
**Very Brief Intro:** A high-contrast motion system built around sound waves, modular LED-like counters, and industrial diagnostics. It emphasizes listening, response, and the sense of a system constantly interpreting the room.

**Probability:** 0.09

## Chosen Direction — Orbital Command Atelier

### Design Movement
The interface follows **neo-futurist control-room minimalism**: precise operational typography, orbital diagrams, and high-quality technical instrument panels with a restrained amount of glow.

### Core Principles
1. Treat the dashboard as a calm command desk, not a generic chatbot.
2. Give voice interaction visual presence through one centered listening orb and subtle signal movement.
3. Create hierarchy through exact spacing, hairline separators, and clustered information rather than rounded-card repetition.
4. Keep potentially sensitive actions visibly governed by permission state and confirmation cues.

### Color Philosophy
Near-black ink and desaturated navy establish privacy and focus. Arthur’s ownable cobalt blue signals attention and controlled capability; pale cyan is reserved for active listening, verified connections, and healthy system states. Small amber marks communicate actions that need the user’s consent.

### Layout Paradigm
Use a **left instrument rail plus asymmetric command canvas**. The primary column is a live conversation and listening surface; a narrow right column houses status and analytics instruments. Settings move into task-specific panes instead of centering the whole page in a card grid.

### Signature Elements
1. A segmented cobalt listening orb with a thin orbit ring.
2. A fine crosshair-grid background interrupted by a luminous orbital curve.
3. Compact status lozenges with a dot, a label, and an explicit state rather than generic badges.

### Interaction Philosophy
Controls should react like precise equipment: short state changes, clear labels, and no hidden consequences. Any mock action that would contact an external service explains that this preview uses placeholders.

### Animation
The listening orb breathes in a 2.8-second loop, while minor telemetry lines drift almost imperceptibly. Buttons use a 140ms press response, panels enter with a 180ms opacity-and-translate transition, and no motion is essential for understanding the interface. All nonessential motion respects reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography System
Use **Space Grotesk** for display labels, navigation, and system values, paired with **Source Sans 3** for readable explanatory copy. Display labels are uppercase with generous tracking; conversational content remains sentence case and comfortably spaced.

### Brand Essence
**Arthur is a composed, multilingual desktop intelligence for people who want capable automation without losing control.**

Personality adjectives: **composed, perceptive, exacting**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are concise and assured. CTAs say exactly what will happen, while microcopy tells the truth about permissions and placeholder integrations.

> “At your signal, Sir.”

> “I can prepare that action. I will not execute it without your approval.”

### Wordmark & Logo
Arthur uses an abstract orbital **A**: three controlled cobalt arcs orbiting a pale-cyan signal point. The mark is used independently of the wordmark in the sidebar and status surfaces.

### Signature Brand Color
**Arthur Cobalt — #2F6BFF**

## Style Decisions

- Arthur surfaces use hairline separators, clipped instrument corners, and asymmetric readout clusters before conventional rounded cards.
- Arthur Cobalt `#2F6BFF` signals primary attention and deliberate action; pale cyan signals listening, verified, and healthy states; amber signals consent or approval-needed states only.
- Utility labels state the actual operation and its consent state, using language such as “prepare,” “request approval,” and “inspect.”
